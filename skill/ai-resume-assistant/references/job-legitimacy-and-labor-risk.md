# Job legitimacy, labor-risk, and recruitment-safety assessment

Use this reference when a user supplies a job post, recruiter message, interview invitation, offer summary, outsourcing arrangement, or asks whether an opportunity is credible, compliant, or worth pursuing.

This is decision support, not a legal opinion, fraud adjudication, or guarantee of an employer. Default to mainland China only when the work and employing entity are in mainland China. Ask or state the jurisdiction when it could change the answer. Internships, civil-service appointments, platform work, overseas employment, contractor relationships, and genuine service contracts may follow different rules.

## 1. Preserve the evidence boundary

Separate these states:

| State | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `observed` | The supplied post, message, contract, or page says it directly |
| `corroborated` | A current official or independent source supports it |
| `unclear` | The information is absent, vague, or cannot yet be verified |
| `inconsistent` | Sources, identities, dates, salary, or duties conflict |
| `officially determined` | A regulator, police authority, arbitral body, or court has made a finding |

Do not label a company or recruiter “illegal,” “fraudulent,” or “a scam” from style alone. Use phrases such as `存在高风险信号`, `可能与现行规则冲突`, `主体尚未核实`, or `需要在提供信息前确认`. Reserve categorical findings for unambiguous conduct or an official determination.

Keep four judgments separate:

1. **Role fit** — whether the candidate matches the work.
2. **Opportunity attractiveness** — pay, growth, scope, location, and tradeoffs.
3. **Labor and hiring risk** — whether stated terms raise compliance concerns.
4. **Identity, fraud, and data risk** — whether the poster, purpose, or information request is credible and safe.

A strong match can still be unsafe. A vague post can be genuine. A high salary is not evidence of fraud by itself.

## 2. Run a source and identity check

Check the following when evidence is available or browsing is authorized:

- exact legal name of the employing entity, not only a brand or product name;
- whether the recruiter represents the employer, an authorized agency, an outsourcing vendor, or an unknown third party;
- whether the employer name, email domain, platform certification, website, phone, address, and interview channel agree;
- current registration and operating status in the National Enterprise Credit Information Publicity System;
- for a commercial recruitment intermediary, its displayed business license, human-resources service license, service scope, fees, and employer authorization;
- whether the same role appears on the employer's current careers page or verified account;
- posting date, update date, application link, and whether an old or copied post is being recirculated;
- whether the labor-contract entity, workplace, payroll entity, social-insurance entity, and day-to-day manager are disclosed and consistent.

An outsourcing or staffing role is not inherently untrustworthy. Treat undisclosed or shifting employing entities as a verification gap. Do not describe an outsourced worker as a direct employee of the client company.

## 3. Check whether the post contains the minimum decision information

Look for:

- employer or commissioning entity;
- hiring type: direct employment, labor dispatch, outsourcing, internship, contractor, part-time, or project service;
- work content and reporting boundary;
- work location and remote/on-site expectations;
- basic pay or an interpretable compensation range;
- working conditions, schedule, rest, and travel/on-call expectations where material;
- contract, probation, social-insurance, and benefits information when offered;
- posting validity or evidence that the opening is current.

Missing information is normally `unclear`, not proof of illegality. Escalate when multiple material elements are absent, the recruiter refuses reasonable questions, or later explanations conflict with the public post.

## 4. Mainland-China baseline screen

Verify the current law and local rules at assessment time. Use official sources and record the retrieval date.

### Recruitment and disclosure

- Recruitment information should be true and lawful. Current rules expect core information such as the employer, hiring conditions, work content, location, and basic remuneration.
- An employer should truthfully explain work content, conditions, location, occupational hazards, safety, and remuneration. A candidate should truthfully provide information directly related to the employment contract.
- A job intermediary or network recruitment service should verify employer materials and protect user information.

### Money, documents, and accounts

Treat these as critical stop signals unless a clearly different lawful transaction has been independently established:

- deposit, guarantee, onboarding fee, equipment purchase, document fee, clothing fee, paid “internal referral,” or payment to secure an offer;
- a training agreement or loan introduced only after the user applies for a job;
- retention of an original identity document or other certificate;
- opening or handing over a bank card, phone card, payment account, company account, or platform account;
- transferring money, advancing funds, clicking orders, receiving and forwarding funds, or “testing” payment flows.

Do not upload identity-card images, bank details, facial or biometric data, credit reports, medical information, or family details merely because a chat account asks. First verify the entity, purpose, necessity, recipient, retention, and secure channel.

### Contract, probation, pay, and social insurance

- A labor relationship should have a written labor contract; if work starts first, current law generally requires one within one month.
- The contract should identify the parties, term, work and location, working time and rest, remuneration, social insurance, and labor protection.
- Probation belongs inside the labor-contract term. Current statutory maximums depend on contract length: generally one month, two months, or six months; contracts under three months and task-completion contracts should not set probation. The same employer and worker should not repeatedly agree on probation.
- Probation pay should not fall below the applicable statutory floor, and “no social insurance during probation” is a strong compliance-risk signal.
- Wages should be paid fully and on time. An employer should not force or disguise forced overtime, and arranged overtime should be handled under the applicable working-hours and overtime-pay rules.

Do not infer compliance because a JD is silent. Do not infer a violation merely because benefits or overtime pay are not written in a short post. Convert silence into interview or offer-stage verification questions.

### Working time

Distinguish the standard working-hours system from approved flexible or comprehensive working-hours systems. Explicit `996`, indefinite on-call, unpaid mandatory overtime, or refusal to state the applicable system is a material risk signal, but a full conclusion may require local approval records, actual schedules, and pay practices.

### Equal employment and irrelevant personal conditions

Flag conditions based on sex, ethnicity, race, religion, household registration, region, identity, marriage or childbirth plans, appearance, or age when they lack a necessary connection to the inherent requirements of the job. Do not assume every age, health, or qualification condition is unlawful; identify the claimed job necessity and seek the current legal basis.

### Personal information

Recruitment-related information collection should have a clear purpose and remain within the minimum necessary scope. Sensitive personal information requires stronger necessity, notice, protection, and usually separate consent. Treat premature collection, unrelated questionnaires, uncontrolled spreadsheets, unknown links, and requests to install unverified apps as data-risk signals.

## 5. Detect credibility and scam patterns

### Critical signals

- any money, loan, deposit, paid training, paid referral, or purchase is required to continue;
- original documents are retained;
- bank cards, phone cards, payment accounts, remote-control access, verification codes, or fund transfers are requested;
- the work involves order brushing, cash handling, account lending, gambling, pyramid selling, or another unlawful task;
- the poster impersonates a known company or uses a materially inconsistent identity.

Recommended action: stop the process, do not pay or provide sensitive data, preserve the post and chat records, report through the platform or competent authority, and contact police promptly when money, accounts, identity misuse, or suspected crime is involved.

### High-risk combinations

One phrase is weak evidence; combinations matter. Escalate when several appear together:

- conspicuously high pay + almost no requirements + immediate admission;
- vague employer + private-chat migration + refusal to use an official channel;
- famous-company or state-owned-enterprise branding + paid “internal route” or guaranteed offer;
- job application that turns into training, financing, equipment purchase, franchise, or customer acquisition;
- salary and title inconsistent with duties, experience, location, or market range, with no credible explanation;
- urgency, secrecy, limited slots, or pressure to submit data or payment the same day;
- interview conducted only by text, no identifiable interviewer, no work discussion, and an offer issued immediately.

### Medium-risk information gaps

- no clear employer or using only a brand alias;
- no work location, remuneration basis, employment type, or reporting line;
- recruiter, contract, payroll, and client entities differ without explanation;
- copied language, broken links, inconsistent logos, free email, or contact details that do not match official channels;
- permanently open or repeatedly reposted role with changing duties;
- broad “one person does everything” scope at a narrow salary band.

These may reflect poor recruiting operations rather than fraud. Ask targeted questions before spending substantial time or disclosing more data.

## 6. Produce a calibrated decision

Use four levels without false numerical precision:

| Level | Meaning | Default action |
|---|---|---|
| `low observed risk` | Identity and terms are reasonably corroborated; no material signal found | Proceed, while reviewing the contract and actual terms |
| `verification required` | Material information is missing or mildly inconsistent | Ask specific questions before interview travel, tests, or data disclosure |
| `high risk` | Several independent signals or a stated term may seriously conflict with current rules | Pause; verify through official channels before continuing |
| `critical stop` | Money, loan, accounts, original documents, identity misuse, unlawful tasks, or impersonation | Stop; preserve evidence and report or seek help |

Always include confidence: `high`, `medium`, or `low`, based on source quality and how much of the process is visible. `Low observed risk` never means guaranteed safe.

## 7. Output template

Return:

1. **Decision** — proceed / proceed after verification / pause / stop.
2. **Three separate risk levels** — labor and hiring; identity and credibility; fraud and personal data.
3. **Observed evidence** — quote or paraphrase only the decisive wording.
4. **Unknowns and contradictions** — do not fill them with assumptions.
5. **Legal or policy basis** — source, relevant rule, jurisdiction, and retrieval date.
6. **Verification checklist** — no more than five high-value checks.
7. **Questions for HR** — contract entity, employment type, pay structure, hours, social insurance, probation, data purpose, or other decisive gaps.
8. **Safe next action** — state what not to pay, send, install, sign, or disclose when relevant.

If the user only asks for resume tailoring, keep this screen compact: report only material risks and unknowns. Do not let generic compliance commentary crowd out the fit analysis.

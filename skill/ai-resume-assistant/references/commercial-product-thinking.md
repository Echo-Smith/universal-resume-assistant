# Commercial Product Thinking for Resume Work

Use this reference for advertising, monetization, membership, subscription, marketplace, paid conversion, commercial SaaS, merchant tools, or business-oriented product roles.

## Evaluate the business system

Map the product across four parties:

1. user value and experience;
2. supply-side or merchant value;
3. platform revenue and cost;
4. ecosystem and compliance health.

Ask which scarce resource is being allocated, what the opportunity cost is, and which party gains or loses from the mechanism.

## Metric layers

- **North-star or business:** revenue, contribution margin, take rate, LTV, renewal.
- **Funnel:** exposure, click, conversion, payment, repeat purchase.
- **Supply:** fill, merchant adoption, budget consumption, inventory health.
- **Guardrails:** retention, complaints, negative feedback, organic conversion, latency, refund and churn.
- **Economics:** CAC, service or content cost, settlement cost and break-even.

Do not claim commercial impact without attributable evidence. A candidate may describe a pricing, subscription or monetization hypothesis as an evaluated proposal, but not as implemented revenue.

## Decision patterns

- Compare local metric lift with global and long-term effects.
- Check cannibalization, attribution windows, cohort differences and post-launch decay.
- Prefer explainable merchant diagnostics, controllability and failure recovery over feature count.
- Treat membership as a continuing value contract: benefits, pricing, conversion, renewal, churn and LTV.
- Reject dark patterns and manipulative cancellation flows as evidence of product quality.

## Resume evidence hierarchy

1. measured revenue or margin change with attribution;
2. verified paid conversion, renewal or merchant adoption;
3. shipped commercial mechanism with guardrails;
4. validated pricing or monetization experiment;
5. documented business model or product hypothesis;
6. theoretical familiarity only.

Label the achieved level honestly. Keyword knowledge such as GSP, eCPM, LTV or pricing psychology is not commercial product experience by itself.

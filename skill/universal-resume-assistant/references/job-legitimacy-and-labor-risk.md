# Job legitimacy, labor-risk, and recruitment-safety assessment

Use this reference when a user supplies a job post, recruiter message, interview invitation, offer summary, outsourcing arrangement, or asks whether an opportunity is credible, compliant, or worth pursuing.

This is decision support, not a legal opinion, fraud adjudication, or guarantee of an employer. Default to mainland China only when the work and employing entity are in mainland China. Ask or state the jurisdiction when it could change the answer. Internships, civil-service appointments, platform work, overseas employment, contractor relationships, and genuine service contracts may follow different rules.

## 1. Preserve the evidence boundary

Separate `observed`, `corroborated`, `unclear`, `inconsistent`, and `officially determined` evidence. Do not label a company or recruiter “illegal,” “fraudulent,” or “a scam” from style alone. Use calibrated language such as `存在高风险信号`, `可能与现行规则冲突`, or `主体尚未核实`. Reserve categorical findings for unambiguous conduct or an official determination.

Keep role fit, opportunity attractiveness, labor and hiring risk, and identity/fraud/data risk separate. A strong match can still be unsafe. A vague post can be genuine. A high salary is not evidence of fraud by itself.

## 2. Source and identity check

Check when evidence or browsing is available:

- exact legal name of the employing entity, not only a brand;
- whether the recruiter represents the employer, an authorized agency, an outsourcing vendor, or an unknown third party;
- whether email domain, platform certification, website, address, phone, and interview channel agree;
- current registration and operating status in the National Enterprise Credit Information Publicity System;
- for an intermediary, displayed licenses, service scope, fees, and employer authorization;
- whether the same role appears on the employer's current careers page or verified account;
- posting date, update date, application link, and signs of an old or copied post;
- whether contract, payroll, social-insurance, workplace, and management entities are disclosed and consistent.

Outsourcing or staffing is not inherently untrustworthy. Treat undisclosed or shifting employing entities as a verification gap. Do not describe an outsourced worker as a direct employee of the client company.

## 3. Minimum decision information

Look for employer, hiring type, work content, work location, basic pay, schedule and working conditions, contract/probation/social-insurance information, and evidence that the opening is current. Missing information is normally `unclear`, not proof of illegality. Escalate when several material elements are absent, reasonable questions are refused, or later explanations conflict with the public post.

## 4. Mainland-China baseline screen

Verify current national and local rules from official sources at assessment time and record the retrieval date.

- Recruitment information should be true and lawful. Current rules expect core information such as the employer, conditions, work content, location, and basic remuneration.
- Employers should truthfully explain work content, conditions, location, occupational hazards, safety, and remuneration. Candidates should truthfully provide information directly related to the employment contract.
- Do not accept deposits, guarantees, onboarding fees, equipment purchases, paid referrals, or payment to secure an offer. Treat a job application that becomes paid training or a loan as a critical risk.
- Do not allow retention of original identity documents. Do not open, lend, or hand over bank cards, phone cards, payment accounts, or verification codes.
- A labor relationship should have a written contract. Contract terms should identify work and location, time and rest, remuneration, social insurance, and labor protection.
- Probation belongs inside the contract term. Current maximums generally depend on contract length: one, two, or six months; contracts under three months and task-completion contracts should not set probation. Repeated probation with the same employer is a risk signal.
- Probation pay must meet statutory floors. `No social insurance during probation` is a strong compliance-risk signal.
- Wages should be paid fully and on time. Employers should not force or disguise forced overtime; arranged overtime should follow the applicable working-hours and overtime-pay rules.
- Distinguish standard hours from approved flexible or comprehensive working-hours systems. Explicit unpaid mandatory `996`, indefinite on-call, or refusal to state the system is material, but a full conclusion may require actual schedules and approval records.
- Flag sex, ethnicity, race, religion, household registration, region, identity, marriage or childbirth plans, appearance, or age conditions when they lack a necessary connection to the inherent requirements of the job.
- Information collection should have a clear purpose and remain within the minimum necessary scope. Treat premature sensitive-data collection, uncontrolled forms, unknown links, and unverified apps as data-risk signals.

Silence is not proof of compliance or violation. Convert omitted benefits, overtime, probation, or insurance information into targeted verification questions.

## 5. Credibility and scam patterns

### Critical stop

- money, a loan, deposit, paid training, paid referral, or a purchase is required;
- original documents are retained;
- bank cards, phone cards, payment accounts, remote access, codes, or transfers are requested;
- the work involves order brushing, fund forwarding, account lending, gambling, pyramid selling, or another unlawful task;
- the poster impersonates a known company or uses a materially inconsistent identity.

Stop, preserve records, report through the platform or competent authority, and contact police promptly when money, accounts, identity misuse, or suspected crime is involved.

### High-risk combinations

Escalate when several independent signals appear together:

- conspicuously high pay + almost no requirements + immediate admission;
- vague employer + private-chat migration + refusal to use an official channel;
- famous-company branding + paid “internal route” or guaranteed offer;
- job application turning into training, financing, equipment purchase, franchise, or customer acquisition;
- salary/title inconsistent with duties, experience, location, or market range without explanation;
- urgency, secrecy, limited slots, or same-day pressure to send data or payment;
- text-only interview, no identifiable interviewer, no work discussion, and an immediate offer.

### Verification gaps

Flag an unclear employer, missing location/pay/employment type, unexplained entity changes, copied language, broken links, inconsistent branding, unmatched contact details, or a permanently reposted role. These may reflect poor operations rather than fraud.

## 6. Decision levels and output

Use `low observed risk`, `verification required`, `high risk`, or `critical stop`, plus `high/medium/low` confidence. Avoid false numerical precision. `Low observed risk` is not a guarantee.

Return:

1. decision: proceed / proceed after verification / pause / stop;
2. separate levels for labor and hiring, identity and credibility, and fraud and personal data;
3. decisive observed evidence;
4. unknowns and contradictions;
5. current legal or policy basis with jurisdiction, source, and retrieval date;
6. no more than five high-value checks;
7. questions for HR;
8. a safe next action, including what not to pay, send, install, sign, or disclose.

For ordinary resume tailoring, keep this screen compact and report only material risks and unknowns.
